# Illustration Prompt 04: Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia & Ciliopathy Biology

## Intended website section

Research Area 4: Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia & Ciliopathy Biology

## Scientific story to communicate

Show the consequence of impaired motile cilia and the compensatory opportunity created by cilia-independent gas-liquid transport during tidal breathing. The illustration should make clear that cilia failure does not necessarily mean zero clearance if mucus remains sufficiently hydrated and mobile.

## Reference concepts from Button Lab work

- Shenoy et al., Journal of Clinical Investigation 2026: tidal-breathing airflow drives cilia-independent gas-liquid transport; rate depends on mucus concentration, breathing frequency, and body orientation.
- PCD collaborative grant: ciliary motility, mucus concentration, gravity-controlled transport, racetrack cultures, and cilia-deficient models.
- Rogers et al., Frontiers in Physiology 2022: regional differences in upper- and lower-airway mucociliary clearance.

## Primary generation prompt

Create a refined biomedical editorial illustration, horizontal 16:9, showing a branched medium-sized conducting airway in side section with a central split comparison. In the upper pathway, depict healthy coordinated motile cilia as a synchronized field moving a hydrated mucus sheet upward along the airway wall. In the lower pathway, depict primary ciliary dyskinesia: cilia are present but disorganized and largely still, with mucus prone to retention. Then introduce a third, visually distinct rescue path across the central lumen: gentle tidal-breathing airflow in broad curved translucent blue streamlines carries a sufficiently hydrated mucus sheet along the airway surface despite the cilia-impaired region. Show the airflow path as gas-liquid transport at the mucus-air interface, not as violent cough. Add a subtle body-orientation cue through a small tilted airway segment or gravitational vector, without labels. At the far right, show the contrast between retained dense mucus and mobile hydrated mucus, emphasizing concentration-dependent clearance. Use deep navy for airway structure, Carolina blue for airflow and hydration, teal for mucus, muted coral for ciliary dysfunction, and soft green only at restored transport. Warm pale background, upper-left negative space, no text or labels.

## Optional alternate prompt

Create a triptych with identical airway-surface geometry: normal cilia-driven transport, PCD-like ciliary failure with retained mucus, and cilia-impaired airway with tidal-breathing gas-liquid transport moving a hydrated mucus sheet. Make the third panel clinically hopeful but not overstated.

## Negative prompt

Do not show congenital facial features, a patient, a DNA mutation chart, an arbitrary missing-cilia surface, a high-velocity cough blast, or a generic lung disease infographic. Do not imply that gas-liquid transport replaces cilia under all conditions; the mucus must visibly remain hydrated and mobile.

## Art-direction checklist

- PCD side: cilia exist but are dysfunctional/disorganized, not simply absent.
- GLT must occur at tidal-breathing airflow scale, visually gentle and laminar.
- Include concentration dependence through mobile thin hydrated gel versus retained dense gel.
- Keep the focus on conducting-airway clearance mechanics.
