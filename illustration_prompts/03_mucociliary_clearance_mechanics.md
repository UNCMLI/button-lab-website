# Illustration Prompt 03: Mucociliary Clearance Mechanics

## Intended website section

Research Area 3: Mucociliary Clearance Mechanics

## Scientific story to communicate

Show that clearance is a mechanical outcome governed by ciliary propulsion, mucus adhesion to the epithelium, mucus cohesion within the gel, and airflow-driven cough. The central comparison should be a transportable mucus sheet versus an adhesive, cohesive mucus mass that resists cilia and cough.

## Reference concepts from Button Lab work

- Button et al., PNAS 2018: adhesion and cohesion determine cough clearance.
- Goodell et al., Bio-Protocol 2019: peel-force measurements of human airway mucus.
- Rogers et al., Scientific Reports 2018: in vivo mouse mucociliary-clearance tracking.
- Shenoy et al., Journal of Clinical Investigation 2026: cilia-independent gas-liquid transport as a third clearance mechanism.

## Primary generation prompt

Create a high-end scientific 3D photorealistic illustration, horizontal 16:9, arranged as a clean three-stage mechanical narrative across a conducting airway. Left panel: a hydrated mucus sheet rides above upright beating cilia, with a long smooth directional arrow showing mucociliary transport. Center panel: a close-up of the airway surface shows a dense mucus patch attached to the epithelial surface where the cilia are completely collapsed under the mucus. Make the mucus  internally cohesive, with two distinct force motifs: an adhesive attachment at the epithelial interface and an internal mesh tension within the mucus, but don't add any words. Right panel: a brief high-velocity cough airflow pulse sweeps across the lumen; on the healthy upper trajectory, mobile mucus moves out of the field, while on the lower diseased trajectory an adhesive cohesive mucus mass barely deforms and remains attached. This should be shown as the complete removal of the mucus layer. Keep cilia, mucus, airway wall, and airflow anatomically coherent. Use navy and Carolina blue for normal transport, dense dark teal for mucus, muted coral for pathological resistance, and pale gold for the cough pulse. Use warm off-white background with empty upper-right space for webpage text. No text, labels, arrows with words, logos, or cartoon lungs.

## Negative prompt

Do not use a person coughing, a face, chest X-rays, visible pathogens, green mucus, or an unrealistic tornado in the airway. Do not depict cough as the only clearance mode. Do not conflate viscosity with adhesion and cohesion.

## Art-direction checklist

- Distinguish mucus adhesion to the surface from cohesion within mucus.
- Cough airflow should pass over the mucus, not through an impossible open airway wall.
- The healthy and resistant outcomes must be clear without explanatory text.
- Retain a research-instrument quality, not a patient-education cartoon aesthetic.
