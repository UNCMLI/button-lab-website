# Illustration Prompt 08: Computational & Mathematical Models

## Intended website section

Research Area 8: Computational & Mathematical Models

## Scientific story to communicate

Show a multiscale mechanochemical model that connects epithelial ion transport and nucleotide signaling to water flux, mucus concentration, ciliary transport, airflow, and experimental validation. The image should feel like a scientific model informed by real airway experiments, not an abstract artificial-intelligence network.

## Reference concepts from Button Lab work

- Herschlag et al., Journal of Theoretical Biology 2013: mechanochemical autoregulation of airway-surface-layer volume.
- Wu et al., Journal of Theoretical Biology 2018: integrated epithelial-cell model of airway-surface-liquid regulation by mechanical forces.
- Garcia et al., Subcellular Biochemistry 2011 and Zuo et al., Journal of Biological Chemistry 2008: extracellular ATP/adenosine regulation models.
- Button Lab program: model ion transport, osmotic water flow, mucus concentration, ciliary transport, cough, and gas-liquid transport across scales.

## Primary generation prompt

Create a sophisticated horizontal 16:9 scientific editorial illustration showing a multiscale airway-physics model. Build the composition around a central conducting-airway epithelial cross section with cilia, mucus gel, and airway-surface liquid. Around it, arrange three visually connected but uncluttered domains: at lower left, a cellular membrane microview with stylized ion transport and nucleotide signaling represented by small clean particles and directional flux lines; in the center, water movement and osmotic balance regulate the height of the periciliary and mucus layers; at upper right, a simplified airway-level transport view shows cilia and gentle airflow moving mucus along a branching airway. Overlay a small translucent field of smooth contour lines or a mesh grid that wraps around the system, suggesting quantitative simulation and parameter prediction without equations or readable data. At the far right, return the model to experimental validation: a microscope-like field with tracked beads moving along mucus, shown as a clean observation inset. Use navy for structural model lines, Carolina blue for water and flux, teal for mucus, pale gold for measured signals, and off-white background. Keep the image elegant, coherent, and nontechnical enough for a public research webpage. Preserve upper-left negative space. No text, code, equations, dashboards, AI brains, circuit boards, or logos.

## Optional alternate prompt

Create an isometric airway-on-a-chip concept: epithelial cells and mucus in the foreground, parameterized water/ion flux lines in the middle, and a small simulated branching-airway network in the background. Link these layers with clean geometric paths to show model-to-experiment integration.

## Negative prompt

Do not depict neural networks, a humanoid robot, a laptop screen filled with code, colorful meaningless graph lines, molecules unrelated to airway physiology, or an engineering blueprint with unreadable text. Avoid a generic technology-stock-image look.

## Art-direction checklist

- Multiscale logic must be apparent: cell, airway surface, organ-level transport, experiment.
- The model needs real biological anchors: cilia, mucus, ASL, ion/water flux.
- Contours/grids should support the biology, not obscure it.
- The validation inset should suggest tracked transport data without showing charts or labels.
