# Illustration Prompt 07: Dry Air & Airway Health

## Intended website section

Research Area 7: Dry Air & Airway Health

## Scientific story to communicate

Show how dry inspired air increases evaporative water loss from the airway surface, thins the protective mucus/airway-surface-liquid layers, compresses epithelial cells, and triggers inflammatory and cough-hypersensitivity biology. Include the possibility of rescue by rehydrating ion-rich aerosol, but do not turn the image into a climate-poster cliché.

## Reference concepts from Button Lab work

- Edwards et al., Communications Earth & Environment 2025: global warming increases the drying potential of inspired air, dehydrates/inflames human airways.
- Button et al., Scientific Reports 2026, in press: heat waves elevate airway inflammation and cough hypersensitivity; rehydrating alkaline endogenous ions relieve this response.
- Mechanistic focus: evaporation, mucus-layer thinning, epithelial compression, ion transport, hypersensitivity, and rehydration.

## Primary generation prompt

Create an elegant horizontal 16:9 biomedical-climate illustration in a single continuous airway cutaway. On the left, show warm dry inspired air entering a conducting airway as broad amber-tinted but translucent streamlines, never as flames. At the airway surface beneath it, show water molecules lifting from a thin blue airway-surface-liquid layer into the airstream, leaving the teal mucus layer visibly thinner and closer to the epithelial surface. The epithelial cells should become subtly compressed and mechanically stressed, with restrained coral signaling glows confined to the cellular layer, suggesting inflammatory and sensory activation without text. At the right, show a fine blue-and-pale-gold therapeutic aerosol mist depositing on the airway surface, re-expanding the liquid layer and restoring a healthier separated mucus gel above cilia. Create a visual progression from evaporation and collapse to rehydration and recovery. Include only a subtle atmospheric context at the far-left edge, such as a small heat-haze gradient, but keep the airway biology dominant. Use UNC navy, Carolina blue, teal, muted dry-air amber, restrained coral, and soft green recovery. Warm pale background, ample negative space in upper right, no text, numbers, maps, people, or logos.

## Optional alternate prompt

Create a two-panel comparison: humidified physiologic inspired air preserves a robust mucus and airway-surface-liquid barrier, while dry heat produces evaporation, epithelial compression, and a thin unstable surface layer. A small lower-right inset shows ion-rich aerosol restoring hydration.

## Negative prompt

Do not depict wildfire smoke, pollution plumes, burning lungs, a global map, a human face coughing, a thermometer, or a generic climate-change poster. Do not show inflammation as infection or blood. Do not use flames in the airway.

## Art-direction checklist

- Evaporation must be obvious and directional from airway surface to inspired air.
- The primary injury is barrier dehydration and epithelial compression.
- Rescue should re-expand the ASL layer, not simply coat the airway with droplets.
- Keep environmental context minimal; this is an airway-mechanism figure.
