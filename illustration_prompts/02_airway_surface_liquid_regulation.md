# Illustration Prompt 02: Airway Surface Liquid Regulation

## Intended website section

Research Area 2: Airway Surface Liquid Regulation

## Scientific story to communicate

Show a dynamic airway epithelial surface as a mechanochemical feedback system: cyclic mechanical stress causes nucleotide release, purinergic signaling, ion transport, water movement, and restoration of the airway-surface-liquid layer. The image should convey regulated volume homeostasis, not a static ion-channel textbook diagram.

## Reference concepts from Button Lab work

- Button et al., Journal of Physiology 2007: cyclic versus constant mechanical stress regulates ATP release and mucociliary transport.
- Button et al., Science Signaling 2013: mechanosensitive ATP release maintains mucus hydration.
- Wu et al., Journal of Theoretical Biology 2018: integrated epithelial model of ASL regulation by mechanical forces.
- van Heusden et al., AJP Lung 2020: inhibition of ATP hydrolysis restores ASL production in CF airway epithelia.

## Primary generation prompt

Create a polished photorealistic 2.5D biomedical illustration, horizontal 16:9, of a longitudinal section through a human conducting-airway epithelium responding to rhythmic breathing-associated mechanical stress. Center the image on ciliated epithelial cells with a thin blue airway-surface-liquid layer and a smooth teal mucus gel above it. Along the lower cell membrane, show a subtle rhythmic compression-and-release wave from breathing, followed by small luminous extracellular nucleotide particles released onto the apical surface. These activate a clean, abstract purinergic signaling halo at the epithelial membrane, opening stylized chloride and calcium-activated anion channels while reducing net sodium-water absorption; use directional arrows to show anions and water moving into the airway surface liquid. At the right side, show the restored, appropriately deep liquid layer supporting upright cilia and smooth mucus transport. At the far left, show a slightly depleted surface layer before the response, but do not make it a disease scene. The composition should read left-to-right as mechanical stimulus, nucleotide signaling, ion and water flux, restored hydration, and mucus transport. Use deep UNC navy, Carolina blue, translucent aqua, restrained gold for signaling, and a warm off-white background. Preserve negative space in the upper left. No text, labels, channel names, equations, logos, or generic molecular clutter.

## Optional alternate prompt

Create an elegant close-up of a differentiated airway epithelial surface under a tidal-breathing deformation wave. Show the epithelium as a living sensor: transient ATP release on the apical side, ion and water movement into a re-expanded airway-surface-liquid layer, and cilia resuming efficient mucus transport. Make mechanical force and fluid homeostasis visually inseparable.

## Negative prompt

Do not make this a kidney-tubule figure, an electrical circuit diagram, a generic cell membrane with oversized proteins, a blood-vessel scene, an alveolar image, or a CFTR-only advertisement. Avoid readable labels and glowing neon colors.

## Art-direction checklist

- Show a thin but distinct ASL layer, not an airway filled with water.
- Make the stimulus visibly mechanical and cyclic.
- ATP/purinergic signaling should be apical, not intracellular-only.
- The endpoint is restored mucus transport, not merely open channels.
